2600: The Hacker Quarterly, Autumn 2007

by 2600: The Hacker Quarterly
2600 Enterprises, Inc. · 2007

The 2007 volume, the first with TRANSMISSIONS running in every issue and the first in which web application security takes as much space as the telephone network.

Spring runs "Understanding Web Application Security," "RFID: Radio Freak-me-out Identification" and "Avoiding Internet Filtering," with "Hacking Your Own Front Door," "Dorking the DoorKing" and "Hacking My Ambulance." "Network Ninjitsu: Bypassing Firewalls and Web Filters" and "How to Cripple the FBI" close the issue. Summer covers "Discovering Vulns," "Enhancing Nortel IP Phones with Open Source Software," "Deobfuscation," "Internet Archaeology" and "Java Reverse Engineering." Autumn prints "VoIP Security: Shit or Get Off the POTS," "Social Engineering and Pretexts," "The RIAA's War on Terror," "Hacking an Election" and "The Trouble with Library Records." Winter adds "Building Your Own Networks," "Darknets," "An Introduction to Beige Boxing," "Hacking the SanDisk U3," "Facebook Applications Revealed" and "Zero-Knowledge Intrusion."

The Autumn HACKER PERSPECTIVE column is headed "YOU," an open call for the guest essay filled that year by Bill Squire, Mitch Altman and Rop Gonggrijp.
