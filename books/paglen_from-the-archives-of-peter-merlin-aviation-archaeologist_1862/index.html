From the Archives of Peter Merlin, Aviation Archaeologist

by Trevor Paglen
Primary Information · 2019 · Paperback

Trevor Paglen’s From the Archives of Peter Merlin, Aviation Archaeologist is a stark photographic inventory of fifty-nine objects — challenge coins, patches, models, and other classified-program ephemera — drawn from the private archive of Peter Merlin, the country’s foremost historian of secret aircraft.

Merlin, a former NASA archivist, has spent decades collecting flight wreckage, dossiers, and memorabilia from bases such as Area 51 and Edwards Air Force Base and their surrounding crash sites — often the only surviving trace of programs the government never officially acknowledged. Working from Merlin’s conviction that “something always remains,” Paglen photographed fifty-nine pieces, from the atomic age to today’s drone wreckage, in flat, deadpan close-up, paired with his own captions and an essay on secrecy and military symbology. Primary Information published the book — 150 pages, paperback, edited by Miriam Katzeff, designed by David Schoerner.

Trevor Paglen (b. 1974) is an American artist and geographer — PhD in geography, UC Berkeley, 2008 — whose photography, sculpture, and investigative research have examined surveillance and state secrecy since the early 2000s, from long-lens images of black-budget military sites to Mylar satellites launched into orbit. A MacArthur Fellow (2017) and winner of the Deutsche Börse Photography Foundation Prize (2016), he is represented by Pace Gallery, with work held by the Guggenheim, Tate, MoMA, and the Whitney.
