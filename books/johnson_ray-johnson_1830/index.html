Ray Johnson

by Ray Johnson
Matthew Marks Gallery · 2017 · Hardcover

A monograph on the collagist and mail-art pioneer Ray Johnson (1927–1995), presenting 42 collages made between 1966 and 1994 — most never previously exhibited or published — with a new essay by the writer and biographer Brad Gooch.

Published by Matthew Marks Gallery in 2017, the clothbound volume reproduces Johnson's most expansive work in 87 plates: layered compositions of magazine clippings, photographs, and hand-worked elements that merge art history with celebrity, word with image, and the personal with the universal. Gooch's essay traces a practice delivering, in his words, "surprise and joy, excitement and entertainment," likening the collages to jokes sublimely told by a tortured stand-up comedian.

A Black Mountain College alumnus who turned from painting to collage after moving to New York in 1949, Johnson became a seminal Pop artist, a proto-conceptualist, and the founder of the New York Correspondance School, distributing his work through the postal service — by 1965 he was known as "New York's most famous unknown artist." The library also holds Ray Johnson c/o, the Art Institute of Chicago's 2021 survey of his correspondence art.
