A

The mark system

one component · three sizes · vermilion

The mark is the spines device ruled into a square and stamped in vermilion. It is drawn once and used at three fixed sizes — nothing else, and no piece goes without it. Below 10 mm the wordmark drops away and the seal carries the identity alone.

Rules
  • Square, ruled at 5.8% of its own width, so the frame scales with the mark.
  • Always vermilion. The black variant exists only for the 1-ink fallback.
  • Never smaller than 7 mm — the leaning spine closes up below that.
  • Clear space on all sides equal to one spine width.
  • The seal never sits inside a rule, box, or panel other than its own.
B

The family — Foolscap Mk II

every piece · one mark · black + 朱

Seven pieces, all carrying the same seal at its specified size. The British sheet keeps its foolscap trim, its docket, and its ledger ruling; what it takes from Japanese stationery is restraint and a stamp — the boxed seal, the stacked wordmark, a 34 mm hairline in place of the full-width rule, a confined writing field instead of ruling that bleeds across the page, and the reference set vertically in the left margin. None of it is foreign to a records office, which is why the graft holds.

One bookmark, not five. The collection is ticked by hand rather than printed, so there is a single SKU, a single plate set, and no ink swap — and a new collection never obsoletes the stock. It also puts the bookmark in the same idiom as everything else in the family: a form you fill in.

Why this is the cheaper object
  • Five SKUs became one. No per-collection plate, no ink swap, no guessing quantities per collection.
  • Still 5-up on A4, 10-up on SRA3, straight guillotine cuts.
  • Printing both sides costs a second pass, not a second job — and the verso is the same on every copy, so it never changes.
  • The verso keeps the spine idea: stood in a jar, they still read as a shelf.
  • New collections need a pen, not a reprint. The Other row is the escape hatch.
