Black Photographers

Celebrating the rich tradition of Black photography and visual storytelling.

Featured Photographers in This Collection

James Van Der Zee (1886-1983): A leading figure of the Harlem Renaissance, Van Der Zee was known for his portraits of Black New Yorkers, capturing the vibrancy and complexity of the community.

Roy DeCarava (1919-2009): The first African American photographer to win a Guggenheim Fellowship, DeCarava was celebrated for his intimate and evocative images of life in Harlem.

Carrie Mae Weems (b. 1953): A contemporary artist who works with photography, text, fabric, audio, and video, Weems explores themes of family, gender, race, and class. Her "Kitchen Table Series" is a particularly well-known body of work.

Lorna Simpson (b. 1960): A conceptual artist, Simpson is known for her photo-text works and multimedia installations that challenge conventional views of gender, identity, and race.

Jamel Shabazz (b. 1960): A documentary and street photographer, Shabazz is renowned for his images of New York City's vibrant youth culture in the 1980s.

Kwame Brathwaite (1938-2023): A key figure in the "Black is Beautiful" movement of the 1960s, Brathwaite's photographs celebrated natural Black beauty and style.

Deana Lawson (b. 1979): Lawson is a contemporary photographer whose carefully staged portraits explore themes of family, intimacy, and Black identity.

Dawoud Bey (b. 1953): Known for his large-scale color portraits, Bey's work often engages with history, community, and the representation of African Americans.

LaToya Ruby Frazier (b. 1982): A photographer and video artist, Frazier's work explores issues of race, class, and environmental justice, often focusing on her hometown of Braddock, Pennsylvania.

Nona Faustine (b. 1977): A photographer and visual artist, Faustine's work often uses self-portraiture to explore history, identity, and the representation of the Black female body. The collection includes her book, White Shoes.

Liz Johnson Artur (b. 1964): A Russian-Ghanaian photographer, her work captures the richness and diversity of the African diaspora.

Shaniqwa Jarvis: A New York-based photographer, her work often focuses on portraiture and fashion. The collection includes her book, This Charming Man.

Adrian Octavius Walker: A photographer whose work often explores themes of Black identity and community. The collection includes his self-published book, My Lens Our Ferguson.

Awol Erizku (b. 1988): An Ethiopian-American artist, his work engages with art history, hip-hop culture, and the representation of Blackness.

Dana Lixenberg (b. 1964): A Dutch photographer, her work includes the long-term project "Imperial Courts," which documents the residents of a housing project in Watts, Los Angeles.

Hank Willis Thomas (b. 1976): A conceptual artist working primarily with photography and sculpture, his work explores themes of identity, race, and popular culture.
